## 📌 Overview

Mobile devices—specifically laptops—present unique service, repair, and upgrade challenges compared to desktop PCs. Components are compact, highly integrated, and require specific disassembly sequences and safety precautions. This objective covers servicing core internal hardware, migrating storage drives, and implementing physical privacy and security features.

---

## 💻 Hardware & Device Replacement

### Battery Replacement
* **Internal vs. External:** Older laptop models utilize external modular batteries released by standard latches. Modern laptops primarily feature internal Lithium-Ion (Li-ion) batteries secured by screws under the bottom chassis cover.
* **Safety Rules:** Always disconnect AC power and disconnect the battery cable from the motherboard before servicing internal components.
* **Swollen Batteries:** A swollen battery indicates chemical degradation and poses a fire/explosion hazard. Dispose of it in accordance with local environmental and hazardous waste guidelines.

### Keyboard & Keys
* **Modular Keyboards:** Business-class laptops often feature top-removable keyboards held by captive screws or bezel clips, connecting via a zero-insertion-force (ZIF) ribbon cable.
* **Individual Keycaps:** Individual keycaps and scissor switches can occasionally be snapped off and replaced, though full keyboard replacement is standard practice.

### Random-Access Memory (RAM)
* **SO-DIMM:** Laptops use **Small Outline Dual In-line Memory Modules (SO-DIMMs)**, which are roughly half the physical length of desktop DIMMs.
* **Pin Counts:** DDR4 SO-DIMMs use **260 pins**, while DDR5 SO-DIMMs use **262 pins**.
* **Installation:** Insert at a **45-degree angle** into the slot, then press down flat until side retaining metal arms snap into position.
* **Onboard RAM:** Some ultra-thin laptops feature soldered (non-upgradable) RAM modules.

### Hard Disk Drive (HDD) / Solid-State Drive (SSD)
* **Form Factors:**
    * **2.5-inch Drives:** SATA HDDs or SATA SSDs (7mm or 9.5mm thickness).
    * **M.2 SSDs:** High-performance NVMe (PCIe) or SATA drives. Common size is **2280** (22mm wide by 80mm long).
* **HDD/SSD Migration:**
    * **Cloning:** Creating a bit-for-bit mirror of an existing drive onto a new SSD using disk cloning tools and an external USB adapter. Preserves the OS, applications, and settings.
    * **Clean Install:** Installing a fresh operating system onto the new drive and restoring user data manually.

### Wireless Cards
* **Form Factor:** Typically **M.2 2230** form factor (22mm wide by 30mm long) or Mini PCIe.
* **Antenna Cables:** Connected via small U.FL miniature coaxial connectors. Main (black) and Auxiliary (white) antenna wires run through the hinge into the display panel bezel for high reception.

## 🔒 Physical Privacy & Security Components

### Biometrics
Biometric devices measure biological characteristics to authenticate user access.

* **Fingerprint Readers:** Embedded in palm rests, touchpads, or integrated into power buttons.
* **Facial Recognition:** Works with specialized Infrared (IR) cameras (e.g., Windows Hello) to verify identity regardless of ambient lighting conditions.

### Near-Field Scanner Features
* **NFC / RFID Readers:** Built-in proximity scanners under the palm rest or chassis. Enables tap-to-authenticate functionality using employee smart badges, security tokens, or tap-to-pay devices.
* **Privacy Filters:** Physical optical screens or built-in electronic toggles (e.g., HP Sure View) that narrow horizontal viewing angles to defeat visual eavesdropping ("shoulder surfing").

## ⚡ Key Vocabulary

SO-DIMM
: Small Outline Dual In-line Memory Module. Compact memory form factor used in laptops.

ZIF Connector
: Zero Insertion Force connector. A ribbon cable connector that uses a locking latch to secure thin flex cables without requiring force.

Disk Cloning
: Creating a sector-by-sector duplicate of a hard drive onto another drive.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Pitfalls

!!! exam "Catches on the Exam: Antenna Connectors"
    When swapping a laptop wireless card, if the technician forgets to attach the tiny main/auxiliary antenna leads, the card will be recognized by the OS but will experience almost zero Wi-Fi range.

!!! danger "Disconnect Power First"
    Always unplug the AC adapter AND disconnect/remove the internal battery prior to servicing any internal hardware. Working on live components can permanently damage motherboard circuits.

!!! tip "SO-DIMM Insertion Method"
    Remember: Insert SO-DIMMs at roughly a **45-degree angle** until fully seated in the slot contacts, then press flat until side metal latches click into place.

!!! warning "NVMe vs. SATA M.2 Compatibility"
    Not all M.2 slots support NVMe drives. Check the motherboard specifications to ensure the slot supports PCIe/NVMe before upgrading a SATA M.2 SSD.

!!! example "Scenario: Drive Migration"
    **Scenario:** A technician needs to upgrade a user's slow mechanical hard drive to a high-speed SSD without losing any application settings or user files.
    
    **Solution:** Connect the new SSD using a USB-to-SATA adapter. Run disk cloning software to copy the drive contents directly. Shutdown, disconnect power/battery, replace the old drive with the new SSD, and boot into Windows seamlessly.
