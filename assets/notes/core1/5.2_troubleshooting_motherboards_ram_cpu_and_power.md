## 📌 Overview

Core system components—motherboards, CPUs, system RAM, and power supplies—form the foundation of computer processing. Diagnosing failures across these hardware elements requires analyzing power delivery, reading POST diagnostic signals (beep codes and LED indicators), identifying thermal issues, and applying systematic component-level isolation testing.

---

## ⚡ Troubleshooting Power Supply & Power Delivery Issues

Power problems can range from complete failure to subtle instability under heavy load.

### Common Power Symptoms & Causes
* **No Power / Completely Dead System:**
  * **Symptoms:** Pressing the power button yields no LEDs, no fan activity, and no noise.
  * **Causes:** Disconnected AC cable, flipped PSU switch, wall outlet failure, misconfigured 115V/230V selector switch, or dead PSU.
* **Unexpected Shutdowns / Random Power-Offs:**
  * **Symptoms:** System abruptly powers off during operation without an error message or blue screen.
  * **Causes:** Overheating CPU/GPU triggering thermal safety shutoff, PSU failing under heavy load (overpower/overcurrent protection), or loose internal power cables.
* **Continuous Reboots / Reboot Loops:**
  * **Symptoms:** System powers on, fans spin briefly, and system turns off and restarts repeatedly before completing POST.
  * **Causes:** Failing PSU, corrupt BIOS settings, incompatible RAM configuration, or motherboard short-circuit.
* **Indicator Light Powered, but No Fan Spin / No Boot:**
  * **Symptoms:** Standby power LED on motherboard lights up, but fans do not spin and system does not POST.
  * **Causes:** Faulty power switch connection, blown secondary power rail on PSU, or short circuit on motherboard.
* **Burning Smell or Visible Smoke:**
  * **Symptoms:** Acrid chemical odor or visible smoke emitting from the system chassis.
  * **Action:** **Immediately disconnect main AC wall power!** Indicates blown capacitors or fried power distribution traces.

## 🧠 Troubleshooting CPU & Motherboard Issues

Motherboard and CPU failures often manifest as boot failures, system instability, or physical physical component damage.

### POST Errors & Diagnostic Indicators
* **POST Beep Codes:** Audible alert sequences emitted by the motherboard speaker when POST fails before initializing display drivers.
  * **Single Short Beep:** Normal, successful POST completion.
  * **Continuous / Repeating Beeps:** Memory (RAM) failure or unseated module.
  * **1 Long Beep, 2 Short Beeps:** Video card (GPU) failure or missing display adapter.
* **POST Test Cards / Diagnostic LEDs:** Motherboard LEDs or 7-segment digital displays that display hex status codes during startup to pinpoint exact hardware initialization failures.

### System Instability & Physical Damage
* **Thermal Throttling & Shutdowns:**
  * **Symptoms:** System slows down dramatically during intensive workloads or shuts down abruptly after several minutes.
  * **Causes:** Failed CPU fan, dried out or missing thermal paste, unseated heat sink, or protective plastic film left on cooler base.
* **System Crashes & BSODs (Blue Screen of Death):**
  * **Symptoms:** Operating system crashes with Stop Errors (`IRQL_NOT_LESS_OR_EQUAL` or `PAGE_FAULT_IN_NONPAGED_AREA`).
  * **Causes:** Corrupt drivers, CPU instability, unstable overclock, or physical RAM corruption.
* **Distended / Swollen Capacitors:**
  * **Symptoms:** Cylindrical capacitors on motherboard bulge at the top or leak brownish electrolytic fluid.
  * **Causes:** Manufacturing defects or voltage spikes. Requires motherboard replacement.
* **Intermittent Device Failures:**
  * **Symptoms:** Integrated motherboard features (onboard audio, NIC, USB ports) randomly disappear or fail.
  * **Causes:** Cracked solder joints or overheating chipset (Southbridge/PCH).

## 💾 Troubleshooting RAM (Memory) Issues

Memory issues cause unpredictable OS instability, file corruption, and boot failures.

### Symptoms of RAM Failure
* **Failure to POST / Continuous Beeping:** System powers on with blank display and emits continuous repeating beep codes.
* **Read/Write Errors & Blue Screens:** Operating system crashes randomly or throws memory management stop codes during memory-intensive operations.
* **Incorrect Memory Count in BIOS/OS:** BIOS or Operating System reports less installed memory than physically present (e.g., 8 GB recognized out of 16 GB installed).
  * **Causes:** Unseated RAM module, dirty gold contact pins, defective DIMM slot, or channel mismatch.
* **System Sluggishness (Pagefile Thrashing):** System becomes severely unresponsive when multiple applications are open because insufficient physical RAM forces the OS to rely constantly on virtual memory secondary storage.

## 🛠️ Hardware Diagnostic Tools

| Diagnostic Tool | Purpose & Primary Function |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Multimeter** | Measures AC/DC voltage, current, and resistance. Used to test PSU pin outputs (+12V, +5V, +3.3V) and verify wall outlet power. |
| **Power Supply Tester** | Specialized handheld device that plugs into ATX 24-pin and CPU power connectors to give quick digital voltage readouts. |
| **POST Card** | Expansion card or USB device that captures motherboard diagnostic hex codes during POST startup before video output initializes. |
| **MemTest86 / Windows Memory Diagnostic** | Software utility that performs exhaustive stress testing on RAM cells to identify bad memory addresses. |

---

## ⚡ Key Vocabulary

POST
: Power-On Self-Test. An initial diagnostic routine executed by BIOS/UEFI hardware firmware upon startup to test core hardware components.

Beep Codes
: Audible diagnostic signals emitted by a motherboard speaker to indicate specific hardware failures during POST before video is initialized.

Distended Capacitors
: Motherboard capacitors that have bulged, ruptured, or leaked electrolytic fluid due to thermal stress or component failure.

Thermal Throttling
: Automatic reduction of CPU clock speed executed by system hardware to lower temperatures and prevent permanent heat damage.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Pitfalls

!!! exam "Exam Tip: Testing Power Outputs"
    * **Power Supply Tester:** Fast, easy tool to verify all power rails (+12V, +5V, +3.3V) are functioning.
    * **Multimeter:** Required if precise pin-by-pin DC voltage verification is needed.

!!! danger "Common Pitfall: Smoke or Burning Smell"
    If an exam question mentions a **burning smell or smoke** coming from a PC case, the **immediate first step** is always to **unplug the AC power cord**! Do not attempt to run software diagnostics or check BIOS settings while power is connected.

!!! tip "Remember: Unseated RAM vs. Bad RAM Slot"
    If BIOS reports missing memory, power off the system, remove the RAM module, clean the golden contacts with isopropyl alcohol, and reseat it firmly. If the issue persists, swap the RAM stick into a known-good DIMM slot to isolate whether the memory stick or motherboard slot is dead.

!!! warning "Watch Out: Protective Plastic Film on CPU Cooler"
    A brand-new CPU heat sink often comes with a clear plastic protective sticker over its copper contact plate. Forgetting to peel this off before installing the cooler causes instant CPU thermal throttling and emergency thermal shutdown within seconds of booting.

!!! example "Scenario: Desktop Powers On but Shuts Down After 2 Minutes"
    **Scenario:** A technician replaces a CPU cooler. Upon booting into Windows, the system operates normally for about 2 minutes, then abruptly powers off without any error messages or blue screens.
    
    **Solution:** Abrupt power-off after 1–2 minutes indicates **CPU overheating**. Inspect the CPU cooler to verify the thermal paste was applied correctly, confirm the CPU fan cable is plugged into the **CPU_FAN header**, and verify the plastic protective film was removed from the cooler base.
