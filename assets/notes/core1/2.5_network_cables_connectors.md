## 📌 Overview
Understanding physical cabling, speeds, maximum lengths, and connector standards is a cornerstone of the CompTIA A+ Core 1 exam. You must be able to identify cables by sight, match them to their protocols, and recognize their maximum operating distances.

---

## 🌐 Twisted Pair Ethernet Cabling

Copper twisted-pair cables use pairs of wires twisted together to cancel out electromagnetic interference (EMI) and crosstalk.

### Cat 5
* **Max Speed:** 100 Mbps (Fast Ethernet)
* **Max Distance:** 100 meters (328 feet)
* **Frequency:** 100 MHz
* **Primary Use:** Legacy local area networks (rarely installed today)

### Cat 5e (Enhanced)
* **Max Speed:** 1 Gbps (1000 Mbps)
* **Max Distance:** 100 meters (328 feet)
* **Frequency:** 100 MHz
* **Primary Use:** Standard home and small office gigabit networks

### Cat 6
* **Max Speed:** 10 Gbps (up to 55m) | 1 Gbps (up to 100m)
* **Max Distance:** 55m (10 Gbps) / 100m (1 Gbps)
* **Frequency:** 250 MHz
* **Primary Use:** High-speed networks; includes an internal plastic separator (spline) to reduce crosstalk

### Cat 6a (Augmented)
* **Max Speed:** 10 Gbps
* **Max Distance:** 100 meters (328 feet)
* **Frequency:** 500 MHz
* **Primary Use:** Enterprise 10GbE backbones; features heavier shielding around wire pairs

---

### Shielding Specifications
* **UTP (Unshielded Twisted Pair):** Flexible, low-cost cable with no internal foil shielding. Common in residential environments, but susceptible to EMI.
* **STP (Shielded Twisted Pair):** Features foil wrapping around individual wire pairs or the outer bundle to block heavy electrical noise (e.g., factories, fluorescent lighting, power lines).

## 🔌 Copper Connectors & Coaxial

* **RJ-45:** 8-position, 8-contact (8P8C) modular connector used for Ethernet networks.
* **RJ-11:** 6-position, 2- or 4-contact connector used for telephone lines and ADSL modems.

![RJ-45 vs RJ-11 Connectors](assets/images/cables/rj_comparisons.png)

* **RG-6 Coaxial:** Heavy coaxial cable with F-type threaded connectors; standard for cable TV and broadband cable modems.
* **RG-59 Coaxial:** Thinner coaxial cable used for legacy CCTV analog video signals over shorter distances.

## ⚡ Fiber Optic Cabling

Fiber cables transmit pulses of light through glass or plastic cores. They are completely immune to electromagnetic interference (EMI) and support significantly greater distances than copper.

### Single-Mode vs. Multi-Mode
* **Single-Mode Fiber (SMF):** Small core (~9 microns), uses a laser light source. Supports long distances (miles/kilometers). Used for WANs and campus backbones.
* **Multi-Mode Fiber (MMF):** Larger core (~50–62.5 microns), uses an LED light source. Supports shorter distances (up to ~500 meters). Used inside data centers and local backbones.

### Fiber Connectors
* **ST (Straight Tip):** Round, bayonet-style twist-and-lock connector ("Set and Twist").
* **SC (Subscriber Connector):** Square, push-pull snap-in connector ("Square Connector").
* **LC (Lucent Connector):** Small Form Factor (SFF) push-pull connector with a locking clip ("Little Connector").

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Pitfalls

!!! exam "Cat 6 vs. Cat 6a Distance"
    Cat 6 achieves 10 Gbps up to **55 meters**, while Cat 6a achieves 10 Gbps all the
    way to **100 meters**. This distance split is the single most-tested distinction
    between the two.

!!! danger "Plenum vs. PVC Cable"
    **Plenum-rated cable** has a fire-retardant jacket (CMP) that emits low toxic smoke
    when burned. It is legally mandated for air ducts, dropped ceilings, and raised
    floors. Standard PVC (PVC/CM) cable produces toxic fumes when burned and is banned
    in plenum spaces.

!!! tip "DVI Variants"
    DVI-D supports **Digital only**, DVI-A supports **Analog only**, and DVI-I supports
    **Integrated (Digital + Analog)**. Connecting a VGA adapter requires DVI-A or DVI-I.
