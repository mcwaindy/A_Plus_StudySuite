## 📌 Overview

Physical network infrastructure relies on copper and fiber-optic cabling to transmit data across Local Area Networks (LANs) and Wide Area Networks (WANs). Understanding cabling categories, maximum speeds, distance limits, connector types, and wiring standards (T568A/T568B) is a critical component of the CompTIA A+ Core 1 exam.

---

## 🌐 Twisted Pair Ethernet Cabling

Copper twisted-pair cabling uses pairs of copper wires twisted together to cancel out electromagnetic interference (EMI) and crosstalk.

### Copper Cable Categories

* **Cat 5:**
  * **Max Speed:** 100 Mbps (Fast Ethernet)
  * **Max Distance:** 100 meters (328 feet)
  * **Frequency:** 100 MHz
  * **Primary Use:** Legacy networks (rarely deployed today).
* **Cat 5e (Enhanced):**
  * **Max Speed:** 1 Gbps (1000 Mbps)
  * **Max Distance:** 100 meters (328 feet)
  * **Frequency:** 100 MHz
  * **Primary Use:** Standard home and small office gigabit networks.
* **Cat 6:**
  * **Max Speed:** 10 Gbps (up to 55m) | 1 Gbps (up to 100m)
  * **Max Distance:** 55 meters (10 Gbps) / 100 meters (1 Gbps)
  * **Frequency:** 250 MHz
  * **Primary Use:** High-speed gigabit networks; contains an internal plastic separator (spline) to reduce internal crosstalk.
* **Cat 6a (Augmented):**
  * **Max Speed:** 10 Gbps
  * **Max Distance:** 100 meters (328 feet)
  * **Frequency:** 500 MHz
  * **Primary Use:** Enterprise 10-Gigabit Ethernet networks and data centers.

### Shielded vs. Unshielded
* **UTP (Unshielded Twisted Pair):** Standard cable without internal foil shielding. Highly flexible and cost-effective, but susceptible to strong external EMI.
* **STP (Shielded Twisted Pair):** Features internal foil wrapping around wire pairs or the overall cable bundle. Grounded to shield against heavy electromagnetic interference in industrial environments (e.g., near power generators or fluorescent lighting).

## 🔌 Copper Cable Connectors & Termination Standards

### Twisted Pair & Coaxial Connectors
* **RJ-45 (Registered Jack 45):** 8-position, 8-conductor (8P8C) modular connector used for Ethernet network cabling.
* **RJ-11 (Registered Jack 11):** 6-position, 2-conductor (or 4-conductor) modular connector used for telephone lines and DSL modems.
* **F-Type Connector:** Threaded coaxial cable connector used for cable TV and cable internet modems (RG-6 / RG-59 cabling).
* **Punchdown Block (110 / 66 Block):** An insulation-displacement connector block used in patch panels and telecommunication closets to terminate bulk solid-core copper cabling.

### T568A and T568B Wiring Standards
Ethernet cables must follow standardized color-code pinouts when crimping onto RJ-45 connectors or punching down into wall jacks.

| Pin | T568A Color Standard | T568B Color Standard |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | White/Green | White/Orange |
| **2** | Green | Orange |
| **3** | White/Orange | White/Green |
| **4** | Blue | Blue |
| **5** | White/Blue | White/Blue |
| **6** | Orange | Green |
| **7** | White/Brown | White/Brown |
| **8** | Brown | Brown |

* **Straight-Through Cable:** Both ends use the same standard (T568B to T568B or T568A to T568A). Connects unlike devices (e.g., PC to Switch).
* **Crossover Cable:** One end uses T568A and the other end uses T568B. Connects like devices directly (e.g., Switch to Switch, PC to PC). Modern devices support Auto-MDIX, making physical crossover cables largely obsolete.

## 💡 Fiber-Optic Cabling & Connectors

Fiber-optic cables transmit light pulses down glass or plastic cores rather than electrical signals, providing complete immunity to electromagnetic interference (EMI), extreme signal distances, and maximum bandwidth.

### Fiber Modes
* **Single-Mode Fiber (SMF):**
  * **Core Size:** Very small core diameter (~9 microns).
  * **Light Source:** High-powered Laser.
  * **Distance:** Exceptional distances (tens of kilometers / miles).
  * **Primary Use:** WAN backbones, municipal networks, and long-haul inter-building links.
* **Multi-Mode Fiber (MMF):**
  * **Core Size:** Larger core diameter (~50 to 62.5 microns).
  * **Light Source:** LED.
  * **Distance:** Shorter distances (up to ~500 meters).
  * **Primary Use:** LAN backbones and enterprise data center server connections.

### Fiber Connectors
* **ST (Straight Tip):** Round, bayonet-style twist-and-lock connector ("Set and Twist").
* **SC (Subscriber Connector):** Square, push-pull snap-in connector ("Square Connector").
* **LC (Lucent Connector):** Small Form Factor (SFF) push-pull connector featuring a retaining latch ("Little Connector").

## 🔥 Cable Fire Ratings (Plenum vs. PVC)

Building safety codes strictly regulate the fire resistance and toxic emissions of installed cables.

* **Plenum-Rated Cable (CMP):** Covered with a special fire-retardant fluoropolymer jacket (such as Teflon) that emits minimal smoke and non-toxic fumes when burned. Mandatory for installations inside **plenum spaces** (air handling ducts, drop ceilings, and raised floors where air circulates).
* **PVC / Non-Plenum Cable (CM / CMR):** Polyvinyl Chloride jacket. Flexible and cheap, but emits dense, highly toxic chlorine gas and smoke when burned. Prohibited in plenum spaces; suitable for open room patch leads and conduit runs.

---

## ⚡ Key Vocabulary

Plenum Space
: An enclosed area in a building structure (e.g., space above drop ceilings or below raised floors) used for environmental air distribution.

Cross-talk
: Signal interference caused by magnetic fields bleeding between adjacent wire pairs inside a cable.

Auto-MDIX
: Automatic Medium-Dependent Interface Crossover. A NIC/switch feature that automatically detects the required cable connection type and configures signals accordingly.

Single-Mode Fiber (SMF)
: Fiber-optic cable with a narrow core that carries laser light over long distances without modal dispersion.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Pitfalls

!!! exam "Cat 6 vs. Cat 6a Distance Split"
    Cat 6 reaches 10 Gbps up to **55 meters** only (drops to 1 Gbps at 100 meters). Cat 6a maintains full **10 Gbps performance all the way to 100 meters**. This distance distinction is one of the most frequently tested cabling facts on the A+ exam.

!!! danger "Plenum Fire Safety Mandates"
    Installing standard PVC cabling inside a drop ceiling used as an air return is a severe building code and fire safety violation. Always select **Plenum-rated (CMP)** cable when running wires through air handling spaces.

!!! tip "Fiber Connector Mnemonics"
    * **ST** = **S**traight **T**ip (*Stick and Twist* / round bayonet)
    * **SC** = **S**quare **C**onnector (*Standard / Subscriber*)
    * **LC** = **L**ittle **C**onnector (*Lucent / Latch*)

!!! warning "Watch Out: T568A vs T568B Colors"
    Notice that pins 4, 5, 7, and 8 (Blue/White-Blue and White-Brown/Brown) **do not change position** between T568A and T568B! Only the **Green** and **Orange** pairs swap positions (Pins 1, 2, 3, and 6).

!!! example "Scenario: Running Cables Near Heavy Industrial Motors"
    **Scenario:** A technician needs to run Ethernet cabling through a manufacturing floor lined with high-voltage arc welders and heavy electric motors that generate intense EMI.
    
    **Solution:** Install **Shielded Twisted Pair (STP)** copper cabling grounded at the patch panel, or ideally run **Fiber-Optic cabling**, which is completely immune to electromagnetic interference.
