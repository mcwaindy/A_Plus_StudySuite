## 📌 Overview

Display devices (monitors, laptop screens, and projectors) are the primary visual interface between users and computer systems. Troubleshooting display failures requires diagnosing physical cable connections, resolution and refresh rate configurations, GPU hardware defects, backlight failures, thermal protection cutoffs in projectors, and panel degradation.

---

## 📺 Common Display Artifacts & Visual Symptoms

Visual distortions on screen often pinpoint whether the failure lies within the display panel, monitor settings, video cable, or graphics card (GPU).

### Panel & Image Anomalies
* **Dead Pixels vs. Stuck Pixels:**
  * **Dead Pixel:** A pixel that receives no power and remains permanently black. Cannot be fixed; requires panel replacement if beyond vendor tolerance limits.
  * **Stuck Pixel:** A pixel stuck continuously on a single color (Red, Green, or Blue) due to a transistor fault.
* **Image Burn-In / Ghosting:**
  * **Burn-In:** A permanent static image etched into display phosphors or OLED panels caused by displaying the same static image at high brightness over extended periods.
  * **Image Retention (Ghosting):** A temporary faint ghost of a previous image on LCD screens that fades over time or after exercising pixels.
* **Distorted Image & Aspect Ratio Mismatch:**
  * **Symptoms:** Images appear stretched, squished, or blurry.
  * **Causes:** Display resolution is not set to the monitor's **Native Resolution**, or the operating system aspect ratio (e.g., 16:9) does not match the physical panel aspect ratio (e.g., 16:10 or 4:3).
* **Incorrect Color Patterns / Color Tinting:**
  * **Symptoms:** Screen displays an overall tint (e.g., strong blue or green hue) or lacks specific color channels.
  * **Causes:** Bent pins in analog video cables (VGA), loose connector thumbscrews, uncalibrated display settings, or failing color wheel in DLP projectors.
* **Display Artifacts / Visual Glitches:**
  * **Symptoms:** Strange shapes, random colored dots, checkerboard patterns, or tearing across the screen.
  * **Causes:** Overheating GPU, failing video RAM (VRAM) on dedicated graphics card, or unstable GPU overclocking.

## 💡 Backlight, Power & Signal Failures

Physical power and backlight failures present severe visual disruptions ranging from total darkness to temporary shutdowns.

### Power & Signal Diagnostics
* **No Display Output (Black Screen):**
  * **Symptoms:** Monitor power LED illuminates, but screen remains completely black.
  * **Troubleshooting Steps:**
    1. Verify monitor is powered on and set to the correct **Input Source** (e.g., HDMI 1 vs. DisplayPort).
    2. Check physical cable connections on both the GPU and monitor ends.
    3. Test the monitor on a known-good PC or replace the video cable.
    4. Boot into **Safe Mode** or VGA mode to rule out corrupt graphics drivers or unsupported refresh rates.
* **Dim Image / Invisible Display (Faint Image):**
  * **Symptoms:** Screen appears completely dark, but shining a bright flashlight directly at the panel reveals a faint outline of desktop icons.
  * **Causes:** Failed LCD **Inverter** (on older CCFL displays) or failed **LED Backlight** power circuit.
* **Flickering Image:**
  * **Symptoms:** Screen flickers rapidly or drops video signal intermittently.
  * **Causes:** Loose video cable connection, excessive cable length exceeding specification (signal attenuation), incompatible refresh rate, or failing backlight.
* **Projector Overheating & Automatic Shutdown:**
  * **Symptoms:** Projector operates normally for 10-15 minutes, turns off abruptly, and illuminates a thermal red LED warning light.
  * **Causes:** Clogged air filters, failed cooling fan, or blocked ventilation ports trapping bulb heat.

## 🛠️ Display & Video Diagnostic Summary

| Symptom | Primary Probable Cause | Recommended Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Faint image visible only under flashlight** | Failed backlight or blown CCFL inverter | Replace backlight assembly, inverter, or display panel |
| **Random screen lines / checkerboard patterns** | Overheating GPU or corrupted VRAM | Clean GPU heatsink, check VRAM, remove overclock |
| **Stretched or blurry icons and text** | OS resolution mismatched from native panel specs | Adjust Windows Display Settings to Native Resolution |
| **Overall blue/yellow screen color tint** | Bent pins on VGA cable or loose connection | Straighten cable pins, tighten thumbscrews, replace cable |
| **Projector powers down after 10 minutes** | Thermal cutoff triggered by blocked airflow | Clean dust filters, verify internal fan operation |

## ⚡ Key Vocabulary

Native Resolution
: The exact fixed physical pixel count (width x height) of a digital flat-panel display (e.g., 1920 x 1080) that produces the sharpest image output.

Inverter
: An electrical component in older LCD screens that converts low-voltage DC power into high-voltage AC power to power Cold Cathode Fluorescent Lamps (CCFLs).

VRAM
: Video RAM. Dedicated high-speed memory on a graphics card used to store image frames and texture data.

Burn-In
: Permanent image discoloration etched into a display panel caused by prolonged static image exposure.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Pitfalls

!!! exam "Exam Tip: Flashlight Test for Backlight Failure"
    If an exam scenario states that a laptop or monitor screen is completely dark, but shining a **flashlight at an angle reveals a faint image**, the root cause is a **failed backlight** or **failed CCFL inverter**.

!!! danger "Common Pitfall: Native Resolution Mismatch"
    If text and icons appear **blurry, stretched, or fuzzy** on a brand-new monitor, do not replace the monitor! Open OS display settings and set the resolution to match the panel's **Native Resolution**.

!!! tip "Remember: GPU Artifacts vs. Cable Glitches"
    * **GPU Overheating / VRAM Failure:** Produces 3D rendering artifacts, random geometric shapes, and checkerboard textures across application windows.
    * **Loose / Damaged Video Cable:** Causes flickering, total signal loss, or missing color channels (tinted screen).

!!! warning "Watch Out: Projector Thermal Shutdowns"
    Projectors generate immense heat from high-intensity bulbs. If a projector shuts off automatically after a set period, allow it to cool down, inspect the **air filters**, and clean out dust buildup before replacing the bulb.

!!! example "Scenario: Troubleshooting a Tinted Display"
    **Scenario:** An employee connects a workstation to a desktop monitor using a VGA cable and reports that the entire screen has a strong yellow tint and missing blue hues.
    
    **Solution:** Power down the monitor, disconnect the VGA cable, and inspect the connector pins. If any pin is bent, carefully straighten it using needle-nose pliers, re-seat the connector, and firmly tighten the securing thumbscrews.
