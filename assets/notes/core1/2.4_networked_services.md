## 📌 Overview

Computer networks span a wide range of geographic scales and architectures, from personal wearable devices to global cloud-connected enterprise networks. Understanding the distinctions between different network types (LAN, WAN, SAN, etc.) and Internet Service Provider (ISP) delivery technologies (Fiber, Cable, Satellite, Cellular) is crucial for selecting and troubleshooting network connections.

---

## 🌐 Network Types & Architectures

Networks are categorized primarily by their geographic scale, administration boundaries, and underlying transit technologies.

### Local & Wide Area Networks
* **LAN (Local Area Network):** A network connecting devices across a single, geographically limited area, such as an office, home, or building. High speed and low latency, usually owned and operated by the local organization.
* **WLAN (Wireless Local Area Network):** A LAN that utilizes Wi-Fi (IEEE 802.11) radio standards instead of physical copper or fiber cabling.
* **WAN (Wide Area Network):** A network connecting multiple geographically distant LANs across cities, states, or countries (e.g., the Internet or enterprise corporate WANs). Typically relies on leased lines from telecom carriers.
* **MAN (Metropolitan Area Network):** A network spanning an entire city or town, larger than a LAN/CAN but smaller than a WAN. Often managed by a municipal government or single large service provider.
* **CAN (Campus Area Network):** Connects multiple LANs across a defined geographic site, such as a university campus, military base, or corporate headquarters.

### Specialized & Modern Network Architectures
* **PAN (Personal Area Network):** A short-range network centered around a single individual (typically within 10 meters / 30 feet). Utilizes Bluetooth, NFC, or USB (e.g., pairing a smartwatch to a phone or wireless headphones to a laptop).
* **SAN (Storage Area Network):** A specialized high-speed, low-latency sub-network dedicated entirely to block-level data storage, connecting servers to enterprise storage arrays via Fibre Channel or iSCSI.
* **SD-WAN (Software-Defined Wide Area Network):** An architecture that applies Software-Defined Networking (SDN) concepts to WAN connections. Automatically route traffic dynamically across multiple transport paths (MPLS, broadband internet, cellular) based on real-time network performance and security policies.
* **MPLS (Multiprotocol Label Switching):** An enterprise routing mechanism that directs data using short path labels rather than complex IP network address lookups, providing guaranteed Quality of Service (QoS) and traffic isolation.
* **mGRE (Multipoint Generic Routing Encapsulation):** A tunneling protocol that allows a single VPN interface to dynamically establish direct secure links with multiple remote branch sites (commonly used in Dynamic Multipoint VPNs / DMVPN).

## 📡 Internet Service Provider (ISP) Types

Internet connections differ based on medium, availability, speed, and latency constraints.

### Wired Connections
* **Cable Internet:** Broadband internet delivered over existing cable television coaxial infrastructure using the **DOCSIS** standard. Shared bandwidth architecture; speeds may slow down during peak local usage hours.
* **DSL (Digital Subscriber Line):** Internet delivered over traditional copper twisted-pair telephone lines. Uses dedicated frequencies so standard voice calls and internet data travel concurrently over the same line without interruption. Speed depends on distance from the central office (CO).
* **Fiber Optics:** High-speed internet using light pulses over glass/plastic fiber strands. Offers symmetrical upload and download speeds, immunity to EMI, and minimal signal attenuation over vast distances.

### Wireless & Remote Connections
* **Cellular Data:** Internet delivered over cellular network infrastructure using **3G**, **4G LTE**, or **5G** towers. Enables high-speed mobile connectivity or Fixed Wireless Access (FWA) for homes and businesses.
* **Satellite:** Delivers internet via orbiting space satellites. Ideal for remote rural or maritime areas where terrestrial cables are unavailable. Traditional Geostationary (GEO) satellites suffer from high latency due to distance; Low Earth Orbit (LEO) constellations reduce latency significantly.
* **WISP (Wireless Internet Service Provider):** Provides fixed wireless broadband to rural homes or remote facilities using point-to-point directional microwave signals from a local transmitter tower to a roof-mounted receiver dish.

## ⚡ Key Vocabulary

DOCSIS
: Data Over Cable Service Interface Specification. The telecommunications standard used to deliver high-speed broadband internet over coaxial cable TV systems.

Block-Level Storage
: A data storage method used by SANs where data is written directly to raw storage blocks, appearing to the operating system as a locally attached hard drive.

Symmetrical Speeds
: Internet connections that offer equal download and upload speeds (common in Fiber optic networks).

Asymmetrical Speeds
: Internet connections where download speeds are significantly higher than upload speeds (common in Cable and DSL connections).

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Pitfalls

!!! exam "Exam Tip: Cable vs. DSL Delivery"
    * **Cable** = Coaxial cable (RG-6 / F-Type connector) using DOCSIS. Shared local neighborhood node.
    * **DSL** = Copper telephone wire (RJ-11 connector). Dedicated line to the central office (CO).

!!! danger "Common Pitfall: High Latency with Satellite"
    When an exam scenario describes a remote site where user video calls or interactive sessions lag severely despite high download speeds, suspect **Satellite Internet** due to long propagation delay (signal travel time to orbit and back).

!!! tip "Remember: SAN = Storage Dedicated Network"
    Whenever a question asks about a dedicated network used for connecting database servers to high-speed enterprise disk arrays using **iSCSI** or **Fibre Channel**, the answer is **SAN**.

!!! warning "Watch Out: SD-WAN Advantages"
    SD-WAN allows organizations to replace expensive, rigid **MPLS** leases with a combination of cheaper commodity broadband connections while maintaining automated traffic routing, security, and redundancy.

!!! example "Scenario: Selecting Connectivity for Remote Construction Site"
    **Scenario:** A company is setting up a temporary trailer at a remote construction site where no telephone or cable broadband infrastructure exists. A low-latency line is required to sync CAD files and run site communications.
    
    **Solution:** Deploy a **Fixed Wireless / Cellular 5G router** or a **Low Earth Orbit (LEO) Satellite system** to establish rapid network connectivity without requiring physical ground cable installations.
