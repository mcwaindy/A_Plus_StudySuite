## 📌 Overview

The Power Supply Unit (PSU) is responsible for converting alternating current (AC) power from wall outlets into steady, stepped-down direct current (DC) voltages (+3.3V, +5V, +12V) required by internal PC hardware. Selecting, installing, and configuring a PSU requires an understanding of input voltage settings, wattage calculations, output rails, efficiency ratings, modularity, and internal power cabling standards.

---

## ⚡ Input Power & Voltage Requirements

Power supplies receive incoming AC voltage from electrical grid infrastructure and convert it to low-voltage DC power.

### Dual-Voltage Switches
* **115V vs. 230V:** North America uses **~115V AC** (60 Hz), whereas Europe and many other regions use **~230V AC** (50 Hz).
* **Manual Switch:** Legacy or budget PSUs feature a red slide switch on the rear to manually toggle between 115V and 230V.
* **Auto-Sensing:** Modern quality PSUs feature active auto-sensing circuits that automatically detect incoming AC voltage and adjust without manual intervention.

### Redundant Power Supplies
* **Dual-PSU Modules:** Enterprise servers and critical infrastructure utilize redundant PSUs installed in hot-swappable bays.
* **Fault Tolerance:** If one PSU fails or loses AC wall power, the secondary PSU seamlessly assumes the full electrical load without server downtime or power interruption.

## 🔋 Power Specifications, Wattage & Ratings

Selecting the correct power supply requires calculating total system wattage and assessing power efficiency under load.

### Wattage Calculations
* **Total System Draw:** Sum the maximum wattage requirements of all internal components (CPU, GPU, storage drives, RAM, fans) and add a **20%–30% overhead safety margin**.
* **GPU Requirements:** High-end dedicated graphics cards (GPUs) consume the vast majority of system power; PSU capacity must accommodate both base draw and peak transient power spikes.

### Efficiency & 80 PLUS Certification
The **80 PLUS** rating system certifies that a PSU operates at $80\%$ or higher energy efficiency at $20\%$, $50\%$, and $100\%$ rated load, reducing wasted electricity dissipated as heat.

| 80 PLUS Rating | Efficiency at 20% Load | Efficiency at 50% Load | Efficiency at 100% Load |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **80 PLUS White** | 80% | 80% | 80% |
| **80 PLUS Bronze** | 82% | 85% | 82% |
| **80 PLUS Gold** | 87% | 90% | 87% |
| **80 PLUS Platinum** | 90% | 92% | 89% |
| **80 PLUS Titanium** | 92% | 94% | 90% |

## 🔌 Output Voltages & Rail Architecture

PSUs step down high AC voltage into specific DC output voltage rails.

* **+12V Rail:** Powers the most power-hungry components, including the CPU, GPU, and cooling pumps/fans. Modern PSUs deliver the majority of their wattage on the +12V rail.
* **+5V Rail:** Powers logic circuits on motherboards, legacy storage drives, and USB ports.
* **+3.3V Rail:** Powers memory modules (RAM), M.2 slots, PCI slots, and motherboard chipsets.
* **-12V Rail:** Legacy output rail used for old serial port circuits or PCI expansion slots (delivers minimal current).

## 🛠️ PSU Modularity & Form Factors

PSU physical form factors and cable designs determine enclosure compatibility and cable management cleanliness.

### Modularity Types
* **Non-Modular:** All cables are permanently hardwired into the PSU chassis. Inexpensive, but unused cables clutter the case and impede airflow.
* **Semi-Modular:** Core essential cables (24-pin ATX and 8-pin CPU) are hardwired; peripheral and PCIe cables plug in as needed.
* **Full Modular:** Every cable detaches from the PSU housing. Allows custom cable routing and easy replacement, but requires verifying cable pinout compatibility.

### Form Factors
* **ATX (PS2):** Standard size form factor used in full-tower and mid-tower desktop cases.
* **SFX (Small Form Factor):** Compact PSU form factor designed for Mini-ITX cases and small-form-factor builds.

## 🛡️ Power Supply Protection Mechanisms

Enterprise and quality consumer PSUs include built-in safety mechanisms to protect internal components from electrical damage.

* **OCP (Overcurrent Protection):** Shuts down the PSU if current draw on any individual rail exceeds safe limits.
* **OVP (Overvoltage Protection):** Prevents voltage spikes on output rails from destroying connected motherboard circuits.
* **OPP (Overpower Protection):** Shuts down the unit if total wattage draw exceeds maximum rated capacity.
* **OTP (Overtemperature Protection):** Cuts power if internal PSU temperatures exceed safe operating thresholds (e.g., fan failure).

## ⚡ Key Vocabulary

Auto-Sensing PSU
: A power supply that automatically detects incoming AC line voltage (115V vs. 230V) without requiring manual switch adjustment.

Redundant Power Supply
: A system equipped with two or more hot-swappable PSUs connected to separate power feeds to ensure continuous operation if one unit fails.

80 PLUS Rating
: An energy efficiency certification verifying that a PSU converts at least 80% of incoming AC power into usable DC power.

Modular PSU
: A power supply with detachable cables, allowing technicians to connect only the necessary leads for cleaner cable management.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Pitfalls

!!! exam "Exam Tip: 115V vs. 230V Selector Switch"
    If a desktop PC imported from Europe (230V) is plugged into a US wall outlet (115V) without flipping the manual voltage switch, the PC will **fail to power on** or experience severe brownouts. Flipping a 115V switch while plugged into a 230V outlet will **instantly destroy the PSU**!

!!! danger "Common Pitfall: Mixing Modular Cables"
    Never mix modular PSU cables between different power supply brands or models! Modular cable pinouts on the PSU side are **not standardized**. Using a cable from a different modular PSU will deliver incorrect voltages to motherboard or GPU pins and destroy hardware.

!!! tip "Remember: +12V Rail Dominance"
    Modern gaming PCs and workstations draw nearly all heavy power from the **+12V rail**. When verifying if a PSU can handle a high-end graphics card, check the **+12V amperage rating**, not just the total advertised wattage.

!!! warning "Watch Out: PSU Testing with a Multimeter"
    When testing DC voltage outputs on a 24-pin ATX power connector with a multimeter:
    * **Yellow wires:** $+12\text{V DC}$
    * **Red wires:** $+5\text{V DC}$
    * **Orange wires:** $+3.3\text{V DC}$
    * **Black wires:** Ground

!!! example "Scenario: Computer Powering Off Under Heavy Load"
    **Scenario:** A user upgrades their graphics card. The PC boots fine and runs basic office software, but completely shuts off without error whenever a 3D game or benchmark application is launched.
    
    **Solution:** The new graphics card increases power consumption under heavy 3D load, exceeding the total wattage capacity or triggering **OPP (Overpower Protection)** on the existing PSU. Upgrade to a higher-wattage 80 PLUS Gold/Platinum power supply.
