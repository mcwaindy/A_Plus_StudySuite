## 📌 Overview

Installing and configuring motherboards, Central Processing Units (CPUs), cooling mechanisms, and expansion cards requires an understanding of physical compatibility, architecture, form factors, and proper assembly procedures. This objective covers motherboard layout and features, CPU architecture and socket types, cooling technologies, and installing add-on expansion cards.

---

## 📐 Motherboard Form Factors & Layout

Motherboards come in standardized sizes (form factors) that determine case compatibility, power connection types, and available expansion slots.

### Form Factors
* **ATX (Standard ATX):** $12 \times 9.6$ inches ($305 \times 244$ mm). Offers maximum expansion slots (up to 7) for full/mid-tower cases.
* **Micro-ATX (mATX):** $9.6 \times 9.6$ inches ($244 \times 244$ mm). Supports up to 4 expansion slots. Fits in both ATX and micro-ATX cases.
* **Mini-ITX:** $6.7 \times 6.7$ inches ($170 \times 170$ mm). Highly compact board with 1 expansion slot, used for small-form-factor (SFF) systems.

### Motherboard Components & Headers
* **Bus Architecture & Chipsets:**
  * **Northbridge:** Legacy chip managing high-speed communication between CPU, RAM, and PCIe x16 graphics (now integrated directly onto modern CPU dies).
  * **Southbridge / PCH (Platform Controller Hub):** Manages lower-speed I/O buses including SATA storage, USB ports, audio, and BIOS/UEFI communication.
* **Front Panel Connectors:**
  * **Power Switch (PWR_SW):** Toggles system power (no polarity).
  * **Reset Switch (RESET_SW):** Restarts the system (no polarity).
  * **Power LED & HDD LED:** System state indicators (**polarity sensitive**; positive $+$ and negative $-$ pins must be aligned).
* **CMOS Battery:** Standard **CR2032** coin cell supplying battery power to maintain system date/time and BIOS setting state when main power is disconnected.

## 🧠 Central Processing Unit (CPU) Architecture & Sockets

The CPU executes instructions and processes system data. Selecting and installing a CPU requires socket and architectural compatibility.

### Socket Types
* **LGA (Land Grid Array):** Pins are located inside the **motherboard socket**; CPU has flat gold pads. Used by Intel and AMD AM5.
* **PGA (Pin Grid Array):** Pins are attached directly to the **bottom of the CPU**; socket has receiving holes. Used by legacy AMD AM4 and older platforms.

### CPU Architectural Concepts
* **x86 vs. x64:** 32-bit (x86) architecture limits system memory addressing to **4 GB RAM**. 64-bit (x64) architecture supports virtually unlimited memory addressing.
* **ARM Architecture:** RISC (Reduced Instruction Set Computer) architecture known for extreme power efficiency, commonly used in mobile devices and modern laptops.
* **Multi-Core & Hyper-Threading:**
  * **Multi-Core:** Multiple independent physical CPU cores on a single chip die.
  * **Hyper-Threading / SMT (Simultaneous Multithreading):** Allows a single physical core to execute two software threads concurrently, appearing as two logical processors to the OS.
* **Virtualization Support:** Hardware-assisted virtualization features (**Intel VT-x** / **AMD-V**) that must be enabled in BIOS/UEFI to run virtual machines (Hyper-V, VMware, VirtualBox).
* **Integrated GPU (iGPU):** Graphics processing circuitry embedded directly inside the CPU package, allowing display output without a dedicated graphics card.

## 🧊 Cooling Mechanisms & Thermal Management

CPUs generate significant heat during operation and require active cooling assemblies to prevent thermal throttling or hardware failure.

### Heat Transfer & Cooling Components
* **Thermal Paste (Thermal Compound):** A conductive compound applied between the CPU heat spreader and the cooler base to fill microscopic air gaps and facilitate heat transfer.
* **Heat Sink:** A block of aluminum or copper fins that passively absorbs and dissipates heat into surrounding air.
* **Active Air Cooling:** Combines a heat sink with a mounted fan to actively blow heat away from CPU fins.
* **Liquid Cooling:** Uses a closed loop (All-in-One / AIO) or custom loop containing liquid coolant, a CPU water block, a pump, and a radiator with fans to dissipate thermal energy.

### Fan Power Headers
* **3-Pin Fan Header:** Controls fan speed by adjusting continuous DC voltage.
* **4-Pin PWM (Pulse Width Modulation) Header:** Uses an extra pin for a digital PWM signal, enabling precise rotational speed (RPM) management based on system thermal sensors.

## 🔌 Installing Expansion Cards

Expansion cards plug into PCIe slots on the motherboard to provide additional functionality or higher performance.

### Card Types
* **Video Cards (GPUs):** High-performance graphics processors installed in the top **PCIe x16** slot. Frequently require 6-pin, 8-pin, or 12VHPWR supplemental PCIe power leads from the PSU.
* **Sound Cards:** Dedicated PCIe x1 audio cards providing high-definition audio inputs, DAC outputs, and optical S/PDIF ports.
* **Network Interface Cards (NICs):** Wired Gigabit/10GbE network expansion cards installed in PCIe x1 or x4 slots.
* **Wireless NICs:** Wi-Fi and Bluetooth expansion cards, often requiring an internal USB header connection for Bluetooth functionality and external antenna attachments.

## ⚡ Key Vocabulary

LGA Socket
: Land Grid Array socket where delicate pins are located on the motherboard itself rather than the CPU.

PWM
: Pulse Width Modulation. A 4-pin fan control method using digital signals to adjust fan speeds dynamically.

Hyper-Threading
: Intel technology that enables a single physical CPU core to act as two logical processors to improve parallel thread performance.

Thermal Paste
: Thermally conductive paste applied between a processor and heat sink to eliminate insulating air pockets.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Pitfalls

!!! exam "Exam Tip: LGA vs PGA Pin Locations"
    Always remember: **LGA** pins are on the **motherboard**. **PGA** pins are on the **CPU**. Touching or dropping a CPU onto an LGA motherboard socket will permanently bend the socket pins.

!!! danger "Common Pitfall: Forgetting Thermal Paste"
    Installing a CPU heat sink without thermal paste or forgetting to peel off the plastic protective film on the bottom of a heat sink will cause the CPU to overheat immediately and shut down due to thermal protection within seconds of powering on.

!!! tip "Front Panel LED Orientation"
    If front panel status LEDs (Power LED or HDD LED) fail to light up after assembling a PC, flip the 2-pin connector 180 degrees. Unlike power/reset switches, LEDs are diodes and require correct **$+$ / $-$ polarity**.

!!! warning "Watch Out: 32-bit OS Memory Limit"
    A 32-bit OS or x86 processor architecture can address a maximum of **4 GB of RAM**, regardless of how much physical RAM is installed on the motherboard. Modern 64-bit operating systems are required to address memory beyond 4 GB.

!!! example "Scenario: High CPU Temperatures After Processor Upgrade"
    **Scenario:** A technician upgrades a desktop CPU. Upon booting into Windows, the system fans run at maximum speed and the CPU thermal reading reaches 95°C under light load.
    
    **Solution:** Power off the system, remove the CPU cooler, clean off any old thermal compound using isopropyl alcohol, apply a fresh bead of thermal paste, remove any protective plastic film on the cooler base, and re-seat the heat sink firmly with even screw tension.
