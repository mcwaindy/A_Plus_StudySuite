## 📌 Overview

Wireless networks rely on Wi-Fi (IEEE 802.11) standards and specialized security protocols to transmit data securely through radio frequencies. Understanding frequency bands, speed capabilities, channel widths, and encryption methods is essential for deploying, configuring, and securing wireless networks on the CompTIA A+ exam.

---

## 📶 IEEE 802.11 Wireless Standards

Wi-Fi technology has evolved across multiple generations, each improving speed, range, and band utilization.

| Standard | Marketing Name | Frequency Band(s) | Max Theoretical Speed | Key Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **802.11a** | Legacy | 5 GHz | 54 Mbps | Reduced interference; shorter range than 802.11b |
| **802.11b** | Legacy | 2.4 GHz | 11 Mbps | Inexpensive; susceptible to 2.4 GHz household interference |
| **802.11g** | Legacy | 2.4 GHz | 54 Mbps | Backward-compatible with 802.11b |
| **802.11n** | **Wi-Fi 4** | 2.4 GHz / 5 GHz | 600 Mbps | Introduced **MIMO** (Multiple Input, Multiple Output) and channel bonding |
| **802.11ac** | **Wi-Fi 5** | 5 GHz | 6.9 Gbps | Introduced **MU-MIMO** (Multi-User MIMO) and 80/160 MHz channel bonding |
| **802.11ax** | **Wi-Fi 6 / 6E** | 2.4 GHz / 5 GHz / **6 GHz** (6E) | 9.6 Gbps | Introduced **OFDMA**; Wi-Fi 6E adds the uncrowded **6 GHz band** |

## 🌐 Wireless Frequency Bands & Channels

Wi-Fi networks operate primarily across three unlicensed radio frequency bands.

### 2.4 GHz Band
* **Characteristics:** Long transmission distance and excellent wall penetration, but crowded by microwave ovens, Bluetooth, and cordless phones.
* **Channels:** 11 total channels in North America (20 MHz width each).
* **Non-Overlapping Channels:** Channels **1, 6, and 11** are the only non-overlapping 20 MHz channels in the 2.4 GHz spectrum.

### 5 GHz Band
* **Characteristics:** Shorter transmission distance and weaker wall penetration, but offers significantly higher data rates and much lower interference.
* **Channels:** Dozens of non-overlapping channels allowing wider channel bonding (40, 80, 160 MHz).

### 6 GHz Band (Wi-Fi 6E / Wi-Fi 7)
* **Characteristics:** Exclusive spectrum for modern devices; eliminates legacy device contention, offering ultra-low latency and maximum bandwidth.

## 🔒 Wireless Security Protocols

Securing wireless traffic prevents unauthorized eavesdropping and network intrusion.

### Security Standards & Encryption
* **WEP (Wired Equivalent Privacy):**
  * **Status:** Deprecated / Highly Insecure.
  * **Encryption:** RC4 cipher. Flawed initialization vector (IV) allows keys to be cracked in minutes.
* **WPA (Wi-Fi Protected Access):**
  * **Status:** Legacy / Transitory standard.
  * **Encryption:** TKIP (Temporal Key Integrity Protocol) with RC4 cipher.
* **WPA2 (Wi-Fi Protected Access 2):**
  * **Status:** Standard Industry Baseline.
  * **Encryption:** **CCMP / AES** (Advanced Encryption Standard). Extremely secure symmetric encryption.
* **WPA3 (Wi-Fi Protected Access 3):**
  * **Status:** Current Modern Standard.
  * **Encryption:** 128-bit or 192-bit AES (GCMP).
  * **Key Features:** Uses **SAE** (Simultaneous Authentication of Equals) to prevent offline dictionary attacks, replacing the vulnerable WPA2 Pre-Shared Key (PSK) 4-way handshake.

### Enterprise vs. Personal Authentication Mode
* **Personal (PSK):** All clients connect using the same shared passphrase (Pre-Shared Key). Suitable for home or small office environments.
* **Enterprise (802.1X):** Clients authenticate with individual unique user credentials (username/password or digital certificate) checked against a centralized **RADIUS** or TACACS+ authentication server.

## ⚙️ Wireless Configuration Options

* **SSID (Service Set Identifier):** The human-readable name assigned to a wireless network. Disabling SSID broadcast hides the name from casual network scans, but does not provide true security.
* **MAC Filtering:** Restricts network access based on the unique hardware MAC addresses of wireless clients. MAC addresses can be easily spoofed.
* **Band Steering:** An Access Point feature that automatically encourages dual-band client devices to connect to the faster 5 GHz frequency instead of 2.4 GHz.
* **Long Distance Wireless:**
  * **Omnidirectional Antennas:** Broadcasts signal in a equal 360-degree sphere (standard dipole antennas).
  * **Directional Antennas:** Focuses signal power into a narrow path for point-to-point connections over long distances (e.g., Yagi, parabolic dish, or panel antennas).

## ⚡ Key Vocabulary

MIMO
: Multiple Input, Multiple Output. Antenna technology that uses multiple transmitters and receivers to transfer more data simultaneously.

OFDMA
: Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiple Access. Wi-Fi 6 technology that divides a single channel into smaller sub-channels to serve multiple devices concurrently.

SAE
: Simultaneous Authentication of Equals. Key exchange protocol in WPA3 that neutralizes offline brute-force password cracking.

RADIUS
: Remote Authentication Dial-In User Service. A centralized server mechanism used in WPA-Enterprise to authenticate network users individually.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Pitfalls

!!! exam "Exam Tip: 2.4 GHz Non-Overlapping Channels"
    Always remember **1, 6, and 11** for 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi deployments in North America. Setting adjacent APs to overlapping channels (e.g., Channel 1 and Channel 2) causes severe Co-Channel Interference (CCI).

!!! danger "Common Pitfall: WEP & TKIP Insecurity"
    Never select WEP or WPA-TKIP as a secure option on exam questions. If WPA2 or WPA3 is an option, select **AES** (Advanced Encryption Standard) or **CCMP**.

!!! tip "Enterprise = RADIUS"
    Whenever a scenario mentions "individual login credentials," "employee usernames," or "centralized authentication," the answer is **WPA2/WPA3 Enterprise** using a **RADIUS** server.

!!! warning "Watch Out: Wi-Fi 6 vs. 6E Spectrum"
    Standard Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax) runs on **2.4 GHz and 5 GHz**. Only **Wi-Fi 6E** adds support for the newly opened **6 GHz** frequency band!

!!! example "Scenario: High-Density Conference Room Wireless"
    **Scenario:** An enterprise company is experiencing severe latency and slow speeds in a large conference hall where hundreds of laptops connect to a single 2.4 GHz access point.
    
    **Solution:** Upgrade to **Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax)** Access Points operating on the 5 GHz/6 GHz spectrum, utilize **Band Steering** to force laptops onto 5 GHz, and leverage **MU-MIMO / OFDMA** to serve multiple high-density client connections simultaneously.
