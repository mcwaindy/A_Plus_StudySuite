## 📌 Overview

Proper data destruction ensures sensitive information cannot be recovered from retired, repurposed, or discarded storage devices. Core 2 requires understanding physical destruction methods, logical wiping techniques, outsourcing considerations, and regulatory/environmental requirements.

---

## 🔨 Physical Destruction of Hard Drives

Physical destruction guarantees data is unrecoverable, even with advanced forensic tools.

### Drilling
* Holes drilled through platters or flash chips.
* Effective for small-scale destruction.
* Prevents mechanical operation and data recovery.

### Shredding
* Industrial shredders grind drives into small fragments.
* Common in enterprise environments.
* Works for HDDs, SSDs, and mobile devices.

### Degaussing
* Uses powerful magnetic fields to disrupt magnetic storage.
* Effective for HDDs only — **not** SSDs or flash storage.
* Renders drive permanently unusable.

### Incineration
* Drives burned at high temperatures.
* Used in high-security or government environments.
* Ensures complete destruction of all components.

---

## 🔁 Recycling or Repurposing Best Practices

When devices are reused or recycled, data must be securely removed.

### Erasing / Wiping
* Overwrites data with random patterns.
* Tools include:
  * DBAN
  * Built-in OS wipe utilities
* Effective for HDDs; SSDs require specialized secure erase commands.

### Low-Level Formatting
* Reinitializes drive structure.
* Historically used on HDDs; modern drives simulate this process.
* More thorough than standard formatting.

### Standard Formatting
* Quick format removes file table but leaves data recoverable.
* Full format overwrites sectors in modern Windows versions.
* Not sufficient for highly sensitive data unless combined with wiping.

## 🏢 Outsourcing Concepts

### Third-Party Vendor
* Specialized companies handle destruction or recycling.
* Provide:
  * Secure transport
  * Chain-of-custody documentation
  * Certified destruction processes

### Certification of Destruction / Recycling
* Official documentation proving data was destroyed.
* Required for:
  * Compliance audits
  * Legal protection
  * Industry regulations

---

## 📜 Regulatory & Environmental Requirements

Organizations must comply with laws governing data disposal and environmental safety.

### Regulatory Requirements
* May include:
  * HIPAA (health data)
  * PCI-DSS (payment data)
  * GDPR (EU personal data)
  * State privacy laws
* Require secure destruction of sensitive information.

### Environmental Requirements
* Electronics contain hazardous materials (lead, mercury, lithium).
* Proper disposal prevents environmental contamination.
* E-waste recycling programs ensure:
  * Safe handling
  * Material recovery
  * Compliance with environmental regulations

## ⚡ Key Vocabulary

Degaussing  
: Magnetic destruction method effective only on HDDs.

Wiping  
: Overwriting data to prevent recovery.

Certification of Destruction  
: Document verifying secure disposal.

E-Waste  
: Electronic waste requiring environmentally safe disposal.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Pitfalls

!!! exam "Exam Tip: SSD vs. HDD"
    **Degaussing does NOT work on SSDs** — use shredding, incineration, or secure erase.

!!! danger "Common Pitfall: Quick Format"
    Quick format does **not** securely erase data. Sensitive data requires wiping or physical destruction.

!!! tip "Remember: Certification"
    If a scenario involves compliance or audits, the correct answer is **certified destruction** via a third-party vendor.

!!! warning "Watch Out: Environmental Laws"
    Throwing electronics in regular trash violates environmental regulations — always use proper recycling channels.

!!! example "Scenario: Retiring Old Laptops"
    **Scenario:** A company is recycling 50 laptops containing customer data.  
    **Solution:** Wipe drives using secure erase, then send devices to a certified recycling vendor for documentation.
