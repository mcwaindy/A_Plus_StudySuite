## 📌 Overview

IT technicians must follow proper environmental safety procedures when handling hazardous materials, disposing of components, and maintaining safe working conditions. Core 2 requires understanding MSDS documentation, proper disposal methods, environmental controls, and power‑related protections.

---

## 🧪 Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS) Documentation

MSDS (or SDS) provides safety guidelines for handling, storing, and disposing of hazardous materials.

### Proper Battery Disposal
* Batteries contain chemicals such as lithium, nickel, and cadmium.
* Must be disposed of at:
  * Certified recycling centers  
  * Battery collection points  
* Never throw batteries in regular trash.

### Proper Toner Disposal
* Toner cartridges contain fine particulate powder.
* Disposal methods:
  * Manufacturer recycling programs  
  * Certified e‑waste facilities  
* Avoid inhaling toner dust; use masks when handling spills.

### Proper Disposal of Other Devices and Assets
* Includes:
  * PCs  
  * Monitors  
  * Printers  
  * Mobile devices  
* Follow:
  * E‑waste recycling laws  
  * Local environmental regulations  
  * Company disposal policies

## 🌡 Temperature, Humidity & Ventilation Controls

Maintaining proper environmental conditions prevents overheating, corrosion, and hardware failure.

### Location / Equipment Placement
* Keep equipment away from:
  * Direct sunlight  
  * Heat sources  
  * Moisture  
* Ensure adequate airflow around servers and PCs.

### Dust Cleanup
* Dust reduces airflow and causes overheating.
* Use:
  * Antistatic wipes  
  * Soft brushes  
  * HEPA vacuums  

### Compressed Air / Vacuums
* Compressed air:
  * Removes dust from fans, heatsinks, and vents.  
  * Use short bursts to avoid moisture buildup.
* Vacuums:
  * Must be ESD‑safe to avoid static discharge.

## ⚡ Power Surges, Brownouts & Blackouts

Electrical irregularities can damage equipment or cause data loss.

### Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS)
* Provides temporary battery power during outages.
* Protects against:
  * Blackouts  
  * Brownouts  
  * Voltage drops  
* Allows safe shutdown of systems.

### Surge Suppressor
* Protects equipment from sudden voltage spikes.
* Should be:
  * Rated for joule capacity  
  * Replaced periodically  
* Often integrated into power strips.

## ⚡ Key Vocabulary

MSDS / SDS  
: Safety documentation for hazardous materials.

UPS  
: Battery backup device protecting against outages and brownouts.

Surge Suppressor  
: Device that protects equipment from voltage spikes.

E‑Waste  
: Electronic waste requiring proper recycling.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Pitfalls

!!! exam "Exam Tip: MSDS"
    MSDS/SDS tells you **how to handle, store, and dispose** of hazardous materials — not how to repair devices.

!!! danger "Common Pitfall: Using Regular Vacuums"
    Regular vacuums generate static — only use **ESD‑safe vacuums** for electronics.

!!! tip "Remember: UPS vs Surge Protector"
    UPS = battery backup.  
    Surge protector = spike protection.  
    They are not interchangeable.

!!! warning "Watch Out: Battery Disposal"
    Batteries must **never** go in standard trash — always follow certified recycling procedures.

!!! example "Scenario: Printer Toner Spill"
    **Scenario:** A technician accidentally spills toner while replacing a cartridge.  
    **Solution:** Use a **HEPA vacuum**, wear a mask, avoid compressed air, and follow MSDS cleanup instructions.
