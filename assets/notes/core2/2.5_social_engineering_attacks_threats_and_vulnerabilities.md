## 📌 Overview

Social engineering attacks exploit human behavior, while technical threats target systems, networks, and applications. Vulnerabilities are weaknesses that attackers leverage to carry out these attacks. Core 2 requires recognizing each category, understanding how they differ, and identifying the correct mitigation strategies.

---

## 🧠 Social Engineering Attacks

Social engineering manipulates people into revealing information or granting access.

### Phishing
* Fraudulent emails designed to steal credentials or deliver malware.

#### Vishing
* Voice phishing via phone calls.
* Often impersonates banks, IT support, or government agencies.

#### Smishing
* SMS-based phishing.
* Includes malicious links or urgent requests.

#### QR Code Phishing
* Malicious QR codes redirect users to phishing sites.

#### Spear Phishing
* Targeted phishing aimed at specific individuals.
* Uses personalized information.

#### Whaling
* High-level spear phishing targeting executives (CEO, CFO).

### Shoulder Surfing
* Observing someone’s screen or keyboard to steal information.

### Tailgating
* Following an authorized person into a restricted area without credentials.

### Impersonation
* Pretending to be an employee, vendor, or authority figure to gain access.

### Dumpster Diving
* Searching trash for sensitive documents, passwords, or hardware.

## ⚠️ Technical Threats

Threats target systems, networks, or applications directly.

### Denial of Service (DoS)
* Overwhelms a system to make it unavailable.

### Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS)
* DoS attack launched from multiple systems simultaneously.

### Evil Twin
* Rogue Wi-Fi access point mimicking a legitimate network.

### Zero-Day Attack
* Exploits unknown or unpatched vulnerabilities.

### Spoofing
* Faking identity (IP, MAC, email, caller ID).

### On-Path Attack (Man-in-the-Middle)
* Intercepts communication between two parties.

### Brute-Force Attack
* Attempts all possible password combinations.

### Dictionary Attack
* Uses a list of common passwords to guess credentials.

### Insider Threat
* Malicious or negligent actions by employees or contractors.

### SQL Injection
* Injects malicious SQL commands into a database query.

### Cross-Site Scripting (XSS)
* Injects malicious scripts into web pages viewed by users.

### Business Email Compromise (BEC)
* Fraudulent emails impersonating executives to authorize payments.

### Supply Chain / Pipeline Attack
* Compromises vendors or upstream systems to infiltrate targets.

---

## 🧩 Vulnerabilities

Weaknesses that attackers exploit.

### Non-Compliant Systems
* Systems not following security policies.
* May lack encryption, proper access controls, or required configurations.

### Unpatched Systems
* Missing updates or security patches.
* Highly vulnerable to zero-day and known exploits.

### Unprotected Systems
* Missing antivirus, firewall, or endpoint protection.

### End-of-Life (EOL)
* Unsupported systems no longer receiving security updates.

### Bring Your Own Device (BYOD)
* Personal devices introduce:
  * Unmanaged software
  * Inconsistent security controls
  * Higher risk of data leakage

## ⚡ Key Vocabulary

Phishing  
: Social engineering attack using fraudulent communication.

Zero-Day  
: Vulnerability exploited before a patch exists.

Evil Twin  
: Rogue Wi-Fi access point used for credential theft.

Insider Threat  
: Attack originating from within an organization.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Pitfalls

!!! exam "Exam Tip: Social vs. Technical"
    If the attack involves **tricking a person**, it’s social engineering.  
    If it involves **exploiting a system**, it’s a technical threat.

!!! danger "Common Pitfall: Ignoring Physical Security"
    Tailgating and dumpster diving are **physical** social engineering attacks — not digital.

!!! tip "Remember: Spear Phishing vs. Whaling"
    Spear phishing = targeted individuals.  
    Whaling = targeted executives.

!!! warning "Watch Out: BYOD Risks"
    BYOD increases vulnerabilities due to lack of centralized control.

!!! example "Scenario: CFO Receives Fake Invoice"
    **Scenario:** A CFO receives an email requesting urgent payment to a vendor.  
    **Solution:** This is **Business Email Compromise (BEC)** — verify sender identity and implement MFA + email filtering.
